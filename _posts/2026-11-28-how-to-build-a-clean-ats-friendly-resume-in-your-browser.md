Recruiters skim a resume in seconds, and before a human sees it, an applicant tracking system often parses it. Fancy two-column designs from free template sites regularly come out of those systems scrambled. [Resume builder](https://bestlatestapps.com/tools/resume-builder/) builds a resume as ordinary text in the standard order, with a few clean layouts, and saves a PDF.

## Steps

1. Choose a template and fill in the contact section.
2. Add experience, education and skills, then optional sections: projects, certificates, languages and links.
3. Reorder rows with **Move up** and **Move down**.
4. Download the PDF.

The preview updates as you go.

## Templates and page size

**Classic, Modern, Compact** and **Sidebar** change the layout. Choose **A4** (India, UK, Europe, most of the world) or **US Letter** (US and Canada). Sending a Letter-sized resume to a UK employer isn't fatal, but it prints with odd margins.

## Built to be parsed

The PDF contains real text in the order a resume is expected to follow: contact, summary, experience, education, then the rest. Any system that reads text can read those sections. You can test it yourself: open the PDF, select all, copy, and paste into a plain text editor. If the order makes sense there, it'll make sense to an ATS.

## Writing the content: what actually helps

**Summary:** two or three lines, specific to the role. "Backend engineer with 5 years in Python and AWS, focused on payments infrastructure" beats "Hard-working professional seeking challenging opportunities".

**Experience:** lead each bullet with a verb and include a result.

- Weak: "Responsible for the checkout page."
- Strong: "Rebuilt the checkout flow, cutting average checkout time from 90 to 40 seconds."

**Skills:** list tools and technologies the job ad mentions, if you genuinely have them. ATS matching often looks for exact terms.

**Length:** one page for early career, two pages for most experienced roles.

**Tailor it.** Keep a master version, then adjust the summary and reorder bullets for each application. Move up/Move down makes that quick.

## Common mistakes

- **Photos, date of birth, marital status:** standard on some Indian resumes, but often discouraged by multinational employers and in the UK and US. Follow the norm for the job's country.
- **Inconsistent dates:** pick one format (Jan 2024 – Mar 2026) and stick to it.
- **Unprofessional email addresses.** Use a simple name-based address.
- **Typos.** Read it aloud, or paste the text into [Text to speech](https://bestlatestapps.com/tools/text-to-speech/) and listen.
- **Outdated contact details,** especially a phone number you no longer use.

## Example

A fresher applying for analyst roles: Classic template, A4, contact, a three-line summary, education (with relevant coursework), two internships with results-focused bullets, a projects section linking to GitHub, and skills listing SQL, Excel and Python. One page. Downloaded, opened, checked by copy-paste. Ready to upload.

## Keep a master copy

Resumes go stale quietly. Every few months, add the project you just shipped, the certificate you finished, and the numbers from your last review while you still remember them. Because entries stay in the browser, it's easy to open the page, add a line and download a fresh PDF. Keep a copy of each version you send, named by company and date, so you know exactly what a recruiter is looking at when they call.

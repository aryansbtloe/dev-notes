A quadratic on paper is a string of symbols. Plotted, it's a curve that crosses the x-axis in two places, and suddenly "roots" make sense. [Graphing calculator](https://bestlatestapps.com/tools/graphing-calculator/) draws functions of x, lets you pinch and drag to explore, and lists the roots and crossings it finds in the window you're looking at.

## Steps

1. Enter one or more functions of x, one per line, such as `x^2 - 5x + 6` or `sin(x)`.
2. Drag to pan, pinch or scroll to zoom, or set the window yourself.
3. Read the roots and the crossing points inside the visible window.

## What you can plot

Polynomials, roots, absolute value, and the usual functions: sin, cos, tan and their inverses, sinh, cosh, tanh, ln, log, exp and sqrt. Implied multiplication works, so `2x` and `(x-2)(x-3)` are fine. Powers use `^`.

Angles are in **radians** until you switch to degrees. In degrees, sin(90) is 1. A whole-number power of a negative value keeps its sign, so `(-2)^3` is −8.

Up to eight curves are drawn at once, and the page lists where each pair crosses.

## Roots and crossings depend on the window

Roots and intersections are found inside the window on screen. If you expect a root and it's missing, widen the x minimum and maximum, or zoom out, and it appears once it's in view. That's also a good habit for exams: always check the graph's full shape before trusting a list of roots.

## Things to try

**Quadratics.** Plot `x^2 - 5x + 6`. Roots at 2 and 3, matching the factorised form `(x-2)(x-3)`. Add `x^2 - 5x + 7` and notice it never touches the axis: no real roots, discriminant negative.

**Solving equations graphically.** To solve `2^x = x + 2`, plot both sides as separate lines and read the crossings.

**Transformations.** Plot `sin(x)`, `2sin(x)`, `sin(2x)` and `sin(x) + 1` together and watch amplitude, period and vertical shift change.

**Growth comparisons.** `x^2`, `2^x` and `ln(x)`: which wins as x grows? Zoom out to see.

**Absolute value.** `abs(x - 3) - 2` makes a V; find its vertex and roots.

## Tips for students

- **Use it to check, not replace, your algebra.** Solve by hand, then plot to confirm.
- **Mind radians vs degrees,** especially for trigonometry homework done in degrees.
- **Zoom in on near-misses.** Two curves that look like they touch might just come close; zooming tells you.
- **Write brackets explicitly** for fractions: `1/(x+1)`, not `1/x+1`.

## Reading a graph like an examiner

When you plot a function, look for five things: where it crosses the x-axis (roots), where it crosses the y-axis (set x to 0), turning points, behaviour as x gets very large or very negative, and any breaks or asymptotes, as in `1/x`. Sketch questions in exams usually award marks for exactly these features. Plot your function, note each one, and you have a checklist for drawing it by hand.

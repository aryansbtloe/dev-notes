The best 25 seconds of a song make a great ringtone; the other three and a half minutes don't. Or you've recorded a voice note for a class, and there's an awkward "um, wait" in the middle. [MP3 cutter](https://bestlatestapps.com/tools/mp3-cutter/) shows the audio as a waveform, lets you drag handles to pick a section, and saves exactly the part you want.

## Steps

1. Open an audio file. MP3, M4A/AAC, WAV and OGG work in all current browsers; FLAC and WebM work in most.
2. Drag the start and end handles on the waveform, or type exact times.
3. Press **Play selection** to hear it.
4. Set fades and the output format (MP3 or WAV).
5. Save and download.

## Keep a part, or remove a part

There are two modes, and the second is the one people don't expect:

- **Keep selection** saves only what's between the handles: a ringtone, a clip for a video, a podcast snippet.
- **Remove selection** cuts out what's between the handles and joins the rest with a short crossfade. A cough, a mistake or a long pause vanishes without an audible click at the join.

## Fades

A clip that starts or ends abruptly sounds cheap, especially a ringtone that cuts in mid-note. A short fade in and fade out smooths the edges. Half a second to a second is usually enough for a ringtone; for speech, a very short fade just avoids clicks.

## Quality

The selected audio is decoded and re-encoded at the bitrate you pick. 192 kbps suits music; 128 kbps is plenty for speech. At 192 kbps and above, the difference from a typical MP3 is very hard to hear. Choose WAV if you'll keep editing the clip elsewhere and don't want another round of compression.

## Making a ringtone

1. Open the song and find the hook. Zoom in on the waveform; the loud chorus is usually easy to spot.
2. Select up to 30 seconds.
3. Add a short fade in and out.
4. Save as MP3.

Android phones accept an MP3 directly as a ringtone. On iPhone, ringtones are added through GarageBand or a Mac, so you'll take the file there.

## Tips

- Type exact times when you need precision. Dragging handles on a phone is fiddly.
- Listen to the selection twice before saving: once for the start, once for the end.
- Only use songs you have the right to use for anything public.

## Finding the exact spot

The waveform is your friend. Speech shows as clusters with flat gaps between sentences, so cut inside a gap rather than mid-word. Music shows loud and quiet passages; a chorus usually stands out as a denser block. Play the selection, nudge a handle, play again. Two or three rounds usually lands it.

## FAQ

**How long should a ringtone be?**
Up to 30 seconds, with short fades.

**Does cutting reduce quality?**
It re-encodes, but at 192 kbps or more it's very hard to hear the difference.

**Can I remove something from the middle?**
Yes, use Remove selection; the two sides are crossfaded.

**Which formats can I open?**
Anything your browser can play.

That interview, parent-teacher meeting or client call is in ten minutes, and you'd like to know now whether your camera works, how you look, and whether the lighting makes you a silhouette. [Webcam test](https://bestlatestapps.com/tools/webcam-test/) shows a live preview with the resolution and frame rate your camera is actually delivering, which isn't always what the box says.

## Steps

1. Press **Start camera** and allow access.
2. If you have more than one camera, or want a different resolution, pick it.
3. Check the readings, take a snapshot, or go full screen.

## Reading the numbers

**Resolution** is the size of the picture the camera is sending right now. It can be lower than the camera's rating: many webcams drop resolution in poor light or over a slow USB connection.

**Frame rate** is measured from the frames that actually arrive. 30 fps looks smooth. Lots of webcams quietly drop to 15 fps in a dim room, which looks choppy and smeared. That's the single best clue that you need more light.

## When the preview stays black

Usually one of these:

- **Another app has the camera.** Zoom, Teams, Meet or a camera app running in the background can lock it. Close them.
- **Permission is blocked.** Check the camera permission in the browser's site settings (the icon left of the address bar), then reload.
- **Physical privacy shutter** slid closed. Many laptops have one now.
- **A camera-off key** on the keyboard (often an F-key with a camera icon).
- **Operating system permissions**: on a Mac, System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera; on Windows, Settings → Privacy & security → Camera.

Then press Start camera again.

## Looking better on video

The camera matters less than people think. Light matters most.

- **Face a window,** don't sit with one behind you. Backlight turns you into a dark shape.
- **At night, put a lamp behind the laptop,** pointing toward you, slightly above eye level.
- **Raise the laptop** so the camera is at eye level. A stack of books works. Nobody looks good from below.
- **Clean the lens.** A fingerprint is the most common cause of a hazy, glowing image.
- **Check the background** in the preview: laundry, a messy shelf, a bright window.

## Mirroring

The preview is mirrored by default so it behaves like a mirror, which feels natural when adjusting your hair. Others in a call see the unmirrored image, so text on your T-shirt reads correctly for them. Switch mirroring off to see exactly what they'll see.

## Phones too

Open the page on a phone and choose the front or back camera from the list. Handy for checking which phone camera gives a better image if you're joining a call from mobile.

## Example: a dim home office

Your camera says 1080p, but the test shows 640×480 at 15 fps at 7 pm. Turn on a desk lamp aimed at your face and the reading jumps to 1280×720 at 30 fps. Same camera, much better picture, and it's obvious in the numbers.

## Privacy

According to the page, the video stays in the page and a snapshot is only saved when you press Download. Google ads appear on the page, as on the rest of the site.

## FAQ

**Why isn't my webcam working in the browser?**
Allow access, close other camera apps, check for a shutter or camera-off key, and try again.

**Why is the resolution lower than advertised?**
Poor light or a slow connection. Add light or try another USB port.

**Can I test my phone camera?**
Yes, choose front or back from the list.

**Why is the picture mirrored?**
To feel natural. Turn it off to see what others see.

A job ad says £40,000. What lands in your bank account each month is a different number, and it depends on where you live, your National Insurance category, your student loan plan and how your pension is paid. [UK take-home pay](https://bestlatestapps.com/tools/uk-take-home-pay/) does the whole calculation for the 2026/27 tax year and shows the employer's National Insurance too.

## Steps

1. Enter the salary, yearly or monthly, and where you pay income tax.
2. Choose your National Insurance category, student loan plan, and postgraduate loan if you have one.
3. Read take-home pay, income tax, National Insurance and employer National Insurance.

## A worked example

£40,000 in England, category A National Insurance, no student loan:

- Income tax: £5,486.00 (20% of the £27,430 above the personal allowance).
- National Insurance: £2,194.40 (8% of the same band).
- **Take-home: £32,319.60 a year**, about £2,693 a month.

At £30,000 in England, take-home is £25,119.60. In Scotland, the same £30,000 has slightly lower tax: £3,451.07 instead of £3,486.00.

## The rules the page uses

**Personal allowance:** £12,570, reduced by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £100,000, reaching zero at £125,140.

**England, Wales and Northern Ireland:** 20% on the first £37,700 of taxable income, 40% up to £125,140, 45% above.

**Scotland** has six bands, starting at 19% and reaching 48% above £125,140.

**Employee National Insurance:** category A is 8% between £12,570 and £50,270, then 2%. Category C (over State Pension age) pays none. Categories B and J have their own rates.

**Student loans:** 9% above the yearly threshold for your plan (Plan 1 £26,900, Plan 2 £29,385, Plan 4 £33,795, Plan 5 £25,000). A postgraduate loan adds 6% above £21,000, and both can apply at once. On £40,000, Plan 2 costs £955.35 a year.

**Employer National Insurance:** 15% above £5,000 for the common categories, shown so you can see the full cost of employing you.

## The £100k trap

Between £100,000 and £125,140, losing the personal allowance creates an effective marginal tax rate of 60%. At £110,000, the allowance has dropped to £7,570. Salary sacrifice pension contributions reduce the income used for the taper, which is why so many people in this band increase pension contributions. Try it in the calculator by adding a salary sacrifice amount and watching tax fall.

## Pensions: how you enter them matters

Enter a yearly amount and choose the type:

- **Salary sacrifice** comes off before tax, National Insurance and student loan.
- **Net pay** comes off before tax only.
- **Relief at source** comes off after tax and National Insurance; higher-rate and Scottish extra relief is claimed from HMRC.

The same £3,000 contribution produces three different take-home figures depending on the scheme, so check with payroll which one you're in.

## What isn't modelled

The calculator uses the standard £12,570 allowance. Marriage Allowance and Blind Person's Allowance are applied by HMRC through your tax code. Payroll uses monthly thresholds, so a single month may differ by a few pence from a twelfth of the yearly figure.

## FAQ

**What's take-home on £30,000?**
£25,119.60 in England, with category A NI and no loan.

**How does the allowance taper work?**
It drops £1 for every £2 over £100,000, gone at £125,140.

**Which student loan threshold applies?**
Your plan's yearly threshold; Plan 2 is £29,385.

**Does it include Scotland?**
Yes, with Scotland's own bands.

## How it works

You choose the country and the document. The page applies that document's published size and head-height rules. Some examples:

- **US passport:** 2×2 inches, head 1 to 1⅜ inches.
- **UK, Schengen and many others:** 35×45 mm.
- **Canada, China and others** have their own presets.

You mark the top of your head and your chin, the guides show the allowed head range, and the photo is rendered at 300 dots per inch.

## Steps

1. Pick the passport or visa you're applying for.
2. Open a photo or take one against a plain, light wall.
3. Drag the guide lines to the top of your head (hair included) and your chin.
4. Fine-tune the position.
5. Download either the single photo for an online form, or the 4x6 print sheet.

## Taking the photo

This is where most rejections come from, not the cropping:

- **Background:** most countries want plain white or off-white. The UK and a few others accept light grey or cream. A plain light wall works; patterned curtains don't.
- **Light:** even and from the front. Stand facing a window during the day. Avoid a single overhead bulb that throws shadows under your eyes and behind you.
- **Distance:** have someone else take it from about a metre and a half away, at eye level. Selfies distort your face and are often rejected.
- **Expression and glasses:** neutral expression, mouth closed, eyes open. Many countries now want glasses off entirely.

The page matches size and head height; light, background, expression and glasses are up to you, so check the official guidance for your document.

## Printing it at the right size

The print sheet fits as many copies as possible on a standard 4x6 inch photo print, with thin cut lines. Order it as a normal photo print at actual size at any photo shop or online service. On a home printer, print at 100% with scaling turned off, otherwise the printer may shrink it to fit the margins and your photos come out too small.

Measure one printed photo with a ruler before cutting the rest. If a 35×45 mm photo measures 33 mm wide, scaling crept in.

Plenty of Sudoku apps interrupt every couple of puzzles with a video ad. [Sudoku](https://bestlatestapps.com/tools/sudoku/) keeps it simple: fresh puzzles at four levels, pencil-mark notes, hints when you're stuck, and your game saved if you close the tab.

## Steps

1. Choose **Easy**, **Medium**, **Hard** or **Expert** and press **New game**.
2. Tap a cell, then a number. Turn on **Notes** to pencil in small candidate numbers.
3. Use **Hint** or **Check** if you're stuck. Finish the grid to see your time.

On a keyboard: arrow keys to move, 1–9 to fill, Backspace to clear, N to toggle notes.

## The rules

Fill the 9×9 grid so every row, every column and every 3×3 box contains the digits 1 to 9 exactly once. That's it. No arithmetic is involved; the digits could just as well be letters.

## Fair puzzles

Every puzzle is generated fresh and checked by a solver, so it has **exactly one solution reachable by logic**. That matters: badly made puzzles force you to guess, which isn't Sudoku, it's coin-flipping. Harder levels simply give fewer starting numbers and require more advanced reasoning.

## Helpful features, used well

**Notes** track candidate digits per cell and clear themselves when you place a digit in the same row, column or box. That automatic cleanup removes the tedious part of pencil-marking.

**Highlighting** shows the selected cell's row, column, box and matching digits. Tap a 7 and every 7 on the board lights up, which makes spotting where 7 can still go almost instant.

**Hints and Check** are there for when you're truly stuck. Try to use Check sparingly: it's more satisfying, and you learn more, when you find your own mistake.

## Techniques that get you unstuck

1. **Scanning.** Pick a digit and look at each box: where can it go, given the rows and columns that already contain it? Easy puzzles fall to this alone.
2. **Naked singles.** A cell with only one possible candidate.
3. **Hidden singles.** A digit that can only go in one cell of a row, column or box, even if that cell has other candidates.
4. **Pairs.** If two cells in a row can only be 3 or 8, no other cell in that row can be 3 or 8. Eliminate them.
5. **Pointing.** If all the 5s for a box lie in one row, 5 can't appear elsewhere in that row outside the box.

Hard and Expert levels need pairs, pointing and sometimes more advanced patterns. Notes make them visible.

## Solving a newspaper puzzle

Stuck on yesterday's newspaper Sudoku? Choose **Enter your own**, type the given numbers, and press **Solve**. You can also play it here with hints instead of jumping straight to the answer.

## Why bother

Short sessions of logic puzzles are a pleasant break from screens full of feeds. Sudoku is also a good one to share with kids learning to reason step by step, and with older relatives who enjoy the newspaper puzzles but find the print too small.

## A gentle routine

If you're new, play Easy until you can finish without hints, then move up a level. Time yourself only after the method feels natural; speed comes from recognising patterns, not rushing.

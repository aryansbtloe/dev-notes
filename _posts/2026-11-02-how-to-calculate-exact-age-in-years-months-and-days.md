Government job and exam notifications in India love age limits: "21 to 32 years as on 1 August 2026". Miss the cut-off by a day and the application is rejected. Working out exact age by hand across months of different lengths is surprisingly error-prone. [Age calculator](https://bestlatestapps.com/tools/age-calculator/) gives your precise age on any date you choose.

## Steps

1. Enter the birth date.
2. Change the **as of** date if you're not measuring from today. Today is the default.
3. Read the age, the total days and the next birthday.

## Checking eligibility for an exam or job

Put your birth date first and the cut-off date from the notification as the as-of date. The result is your exact age on that day in years, months and days.

Most forms want **completed years**, so use the years figure. If the notice says "not more than 32 years" and you're 32 years, 3 months, check the wording; many notices mean "must not have turned 33", but some are stricter. When in doubt, the notice's own examples or FAQ section settle it. Category-based age relaxations (for reserved categories, ex-servicemen and so on) are added on top, and the notification lists them.

## How the calculation works

Years and months are counted in calendar months, not an average month length. If the day of the month hasn't arrived yet, the month count steps back by one. Total days are the exact number of midnights between the two dates.

February 29 uses the calendar's real date, so someone born on a leap day gets correct results in non-leap years too.

Time of day isn't considered; both dates are whole days. If the as-of date is before the birth date, the page asks you to swap them instead of showing a negative age.

## Other ways to see it

The result also lists age in months and days, in weeks and days, total days, and an approximate number of hours. Fun for a birthday card ("10,000 days old!"), useful for paediatric milestones measured in weeks or months.

## Example

Birth date: 14 March 1995. A notification's cut-off: 1 August 2026. The calculator shows 31 years, 4 months, 18 days. For an upper limit of 32, you're eligible. For a limit of 30 with a 3-year relaxation, also eligible.

## Other uses

- **School admissions:** many boards set a minimum age as of a specific date for Class 1.
- **Retirement planning:** exact age on a target date.
- **Insurance and pension forms** asking for age on the proposal date.
- **Next birthday:** shows the date and how far away it is, which helps plan surprises.

## Tips

- Double-check the date format when entering: day and month swapped is the most common slip.
- Use the date on your official documents (birth certificate, Class 10 certificate), which is what recruiters verify.
- Save a screenshot of the result alongside the notification for your records.

## Why manual calculations go wrong

Counting on fingers usually breaks on months of different lengths and on whether the birthday has already passed in the cut-off year. Someone born on 31 January has a tricky "one month later" in February. The calculator follows the calendar strictly, which is also how recruitment boards compute age, so its answer matches what the board will check. It also avoids the common slip of using the application date instead of the cut-off date in the notice.

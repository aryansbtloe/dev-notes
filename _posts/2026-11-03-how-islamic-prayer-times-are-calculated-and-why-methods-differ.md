## How the times are calculated

The times come from the Sun's position, computed with a precise astronomy library:

- **Dhuhr** is when the Sun crosses the meridian (solar noon).
- **Maghrib** is at sunset.
- **Fajr** and **Isha** are when the Sun is a set angle below the horizon, and that angle depends on the method. The Muslim World League uses 18° for Fajr and 17° for Isha; ISNA uses 15°; Umm al-Qura uses 18.5° for Fajr and Isha 90 minutes after Maghrib.
- **Asr** is when an object's shadow equals its length plus its noon shadow (standard), or twice its length (Hanafi).

## Choosing a method

Use the one your local mosque or community uses. The page picks a common default for your region: ISNA in North America, Umm al-Qura in Saudi Arabia, Karachi in India and Pakistan. You can change it any time.

**Hanafi vs standard Asr** makes the biggest visible difference: Hanafi Asr starts later in the afternoon, sometimes by an hour or more depending on the season and latitude. Most communities in South Asia follow Hanafi.

## When your mosque is a few minutes different

That's normal. Mosques may use a different method, or add a few safety minutes to Fajr, Maghrib or Isha. Set their method here and allow for their adjustments. If your mosque publishes a timetable, compare a week against this one to see the offset.

## The month timetable

Open **Month timetable**, press **Show this month**, then **Print**. Useful for Ramadan, when Fajr (suhoor) and Maghrib (iftar) times matter to the minute, for sticking on the fridge, or for an elderly family member who prefers paper.

## Travelling

Search your destination city before you go and print its timetable. At high latitudes in summer (northern UK, Scandinavia, Canada), Fajr and Isha can be extreme or undefined because the Sun barely sets; communities there follow local rulings, so check with a local mosque.

## Why calculations differ between apps

Two apps showing different Fajr times for the same city usually aren't wrong; they're using different angles. The gap between 15° and 18° below the horizon can mean 15 to 25 minutes depending on the season and how far you are from the equator. Knowing which method a timetable uses explains most disagreements, and lets you match the one your community follows.
